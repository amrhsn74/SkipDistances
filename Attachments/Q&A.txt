Q&A

Questions:

1. what channels to communicate with client?

2. could the client add comments with decline?

3. could the client add comments with approval? and if, does it require another approval?

4. who should approve internally?

5. for posting, should it be done using client's business account directly? or post for client as an admin?

6. what should happen for declined posts?

7. who does have the access for drafted/scheduled posts?

8. what should happen for posts that run out scheduled time without approving?

9. who can edit (schedule & post), or delete a scheduled post after approval? does it have a second approval cycle?

10. should it send a reminder for either internal approver or client to respond?

11. who should assign roles and permissions on a campaign/client?

12. for every post, should it be connected to a specific campaign?

13. to initiate a post, should it be connected to client first?

14. can user generate a caption before or without client intake?

15. are there any tools should be integrated with?

16. who can change a brand's rule? does it need an approval?

17. is approval granted per individual post, or per the whole campaign's content plan at once?

18. when something gets flagged (off-brand, non-compliant), can the account manager edit and resubmit that specific item, or does the whole brief go back to intake?


Answers:

1. There's no real messaging in scope. "Client approval" happens on your own review screen, not WhatsApp or email.
2. Your design and yes, add it. Only requirement: a declined item goes back for revision.
3. A comment is fine. If the "approval" actually asks for a change, treat it as a change and send it back through the gate. A plain "looks great" restarts nothing.
4. The internal reviewer the account manager or a content lead. Role name is yours; an internal approval before the client sees it is required.
5. Neither. No real publishing in scope. "Scheduled" just shows in your own store on screen.
6. Not scheduled. Back to draft, revise, re-run through review and client approval.
7. The client's own account team plus the client. Don't show one client's content to another (Clause 0.7).
8. They never become "scheduled" nothing auto-publishes. Keep them as awaiting approval and surface them in your summary.
9. A real edit is a change back through both approvals before it can reschedule. Delete is internal, no client approval, but log who did it.
10. Not required. Stretch, not graded. Your summary already shows what's "in review" and "awaiting client."
11. Your design keep it light. Lean on the account_manager already in the roster; don't build user management.
12. Yes. Every item is produced from a brief, so it belongs to that campaign. Show that link in your ERD.
13. Yes. No content for unknown or inactive clients (Clause 0.6). Client first, always.
14. No. Every piece is grounded in that client's brand guide, no client, nothing to ground in.
15. None. No social platforms, no image/video gen, no analytics, all out of scope. Use the data files and your own store.
16. Editing the guide is your design. The graded part: change a rule and the output must change with it.
17. Your call. Approving the plan as a unit is the natural default. Either way, every item must carry its own status.
18. Just that item, with the rule it breaks. Fix and re-run it. The whole brief only restarts if it's incomplete (that's REQUEST_INFO).
